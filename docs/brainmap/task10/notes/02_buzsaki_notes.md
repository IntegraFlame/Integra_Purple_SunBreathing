## What it says (isolated read)
- **Space and time are not sensed.** No organ senses either; clocks and brains do not "make" time. Physics treats space as the things themselves, with no time "in which" events occur, yet neuroscience still uses the Newtonian theatre.
- **The hippocampus is a sequence generator** (the brain's largest neuronal graph). Space and time *emerge* from learning sequential, relational structure; they are not inputs.
- **Episodic vs semantic:** an episode is a directional trajectory through what/where/when (it has a duration and a place). **Semantic memory forms where many episode trajectories cross the same state**: the junction becomes a context-free node, invariant to the episodes' times and places. Recall recombines by *multiplying the marginals* (what × where × when), like mixing RGB.
- **Transfer time vs processing time:** von Neumann split a unit's work into processing and transfer, dropped transfer, and warned the simplification is unsound for brains. In brains transfer can dominate. Electronics and brains are "time-space" systems: duration is primary; distance is measured as path time.
- **Reader-defined synchrony:** "at the same time" is decided by the receiver's integration window (a pyramidal neuron's membrane constant, ~10–50 ms). Senders whose spikes land inside one window form one assembly for that reader, connected or not; arrivals outside the window belong to another assembly. The same spike train means different things to different readers.
- **Cipher and syntax:** sender and receiver share a cipher; oscillation cycles mark where a message starts and ends; fast cycles make "letters", slower rhythms bind them into "words" (cross-frequency phase–amplitude coupling). Without a reader, neural "information" has no meaning.
- **Distance↔time via speed:** within one theta cycle (6–10 Hz) the spike order of place cells replays the path just passed and the path ahead; larger phase lags = larger distances. Spikes per place field stay constant across running speeds (speed gain compensates).
- **Time-space zoom:** the number of assemblies and spikes per theta cycle is fixed by internal dynamics, so resolution scales with the size of the environment (bigger arena → bigger place fields).
- **Phase beats clock:** cooling the medial septum slows theta; the distance→clock-time mapping changes but the distance→theta-phase mapping does not. Brain "time" is phase of internal rhythms, independent of clock time.
- **One mechanism, many names:** place cells, time cells, episode cells are one internally generated sequence compared against different references (maze arms, rulers, clocks). Coding speed, distance and time separately would be redundant (any one follows from the other two) and would lose precision.
- **Time is a social agreement**, like money: real for communication, without force on things. Time is an abstraction of change. Children learn time through language. Navigation machinery was exapted for memory, planning and imagination (same algorithm for real and mental travel).

## First links to Integra (isolated)
- The Hoard's cognitive-cycle shards are **episodes** (time-stamped trajectories). Semantic Atlas nodes should be **junctions where many episodes cross**: SWDS consolidation can count recurring items across episodes and promote the invariant ones.
- The celestial clock is already a **phase** clock (rotation, lunar and orbital fractions). This chapter says phase is the right primary coordinate for internal processes; civil time is the social contract used to talk to people. Dual stamps = phase + agreement.
- Store **independent** coordinates only; derived ones (e.g. a grid bucket computed from rotation) are labelled as derived, never stored as a separate truth.
- A **fixed per-cycle budget** with resolution that adapts to region size is the theta "zoom": cell depth in the Atlas should follow node density, not a fixed grid.
- "Co-occurrence" of nodes should be judged in the **reader's window** (Rodin's retrieval window, an SWDS cycle), not by emission stamps alone.
