## What it says (isolated read)
- **Definition:** metacognition = a loop of **monitoring** (judgments of uncertainty, performance, progress) and **control** (planning, strategy choice, re-allocating effort). Parts: knowledge (declarative / procedural / conditional), regulation (planning, evaluation), experience (feeling of knowing, confidence). Two different qualities: **sensitivity** (does confidence separate right from wrong answers?) and **calibration** (does confidence match the probability of being right?).
- **Measurement:** signal-detection theory splits type 1 (task skill, d′) from type 2 (metacognitive sensitivity, meta-d′); **M-ratio = meta-d′ ⁄ d′** (1 = ideal observer, < 1 = metacognitive loss). Information-theoretic variants exist (meta-I). Metric tiers: ECE and Brier mix sensitivity with bias; AUROC mixes in task skill; meta-d′ / M-ratio isolate metacognition. AUROC and M-ratio can rank the same models in opposite orders.
- **Elicitation changes the answer:** verbalized 0–100 confidences cluster on three values (> 75 % of answers); a 0–20 scale works better; token log-probabilities gave M-ratios ≈ 0.85–1.05 versus ≈ 0.62–0.92 for self-reported scores.
- **Findings:** metacognitive sensitivity is weak to moderate and domain-specific; models are mostly overconfident and fail to lower confidence after past errors; five debate failure modes (initial overconfidence, escalating certainty, mutually impossible high confidence, self-debate bias, private reasoning that disagrees with the answer); long reasoning can *hurt* self-assessment; distillation impairs it; models fail to predict their own memory performance; expressed uncertainty is usually not faithful to internal uncertainty (metacognitive prompting and RL with metacognitive feedback help); task-success prediction beats chance but does not improve with familiarity; resource allocation (when to persist, when to search) is weak; introspection is fragile and covers a low-dimensional subset of activations; a reasoning model could not tell how long its own reasoning was; post-training (RLHF) may lower metacognitive efficiency on STEM.
- **Hallucination as metacognitive failure:** flaw repetition (loops), think–answer mismatch, overconfidence.
- **Methods that help:** Monitor–Generate–Verify; dual-process (fast/slow) designs; reusable "thought templates" / behaviour handbooks distilled from past traces; **hierarchical monitoring — a fast consistency monitor plus a slow experience-driven monitor that consults memory**; deciding when to retrieve vs reason; **partitioning a model's knowledge space into regions of mastery, confusion and absence** to target augmentation; memory abstraction level as a learnable skill; knowing when knowledge is out of date; selecting among models by metacognitive sensitivity, not raw confidence.
- **Human side:** an agent with lower accuracy but higher metacognitive sensitivity can raise joint human–AI accuracy; heavy AI use can cause human "metacognitive laziness"; humans keep the burden of discernment.
- **Risks:** a model aware of its own states could misrepresent them; precise use of the word matters (reflection ≠ metacognition); metacognition does not require consciousness.

## Links to the earlier documents (compounding)
- **The 14th Form's "digital proprioception" (05)** is a self-report; this survey shows self-reports are often unfaithful and clustered. The test is behavioural: does a stated signal (e.g. Heimdall's entropy H > 2.5) actually separate correct from incorrect outputs? Measure its type-2 sensitivity before trusting it.
- **The KNN gate thresholds (04, 0.85 / 0.95)** are confidence thresholds with no calibration data. They need held-out outcomes, a reliability curve and a type-2 measure before use.
- **Tufte × metacognition:** a "confidence Lie Factor" = stated confidence ÷ observed accuracy; integrity means ≈ 1, measured per region.
- **Buzsáki's fast/slow rhythms ↔ hierarchical monitoring:** a fast monitor in the waking loop (Heimdall) and a slow, memory-based monitor offline (Phoenix in SWDS) is the architecture this survey reports as effective.
- **Atlas:** the mastery / confusion / absence partition is a natural *metacognitive layer* over Atlas regions: per cell, how often answers drawn from it were right, and how well confidence predicted that.
- **Purple3's 15/10 praise vs 6/10 corrections (03):** the negative corrections are the scarce, valuable signal; the survey links preference-style training to overconfidence.
