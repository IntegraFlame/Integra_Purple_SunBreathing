*Objective: Extract structural boundaries, hard mathematical constants, and empirical timeline events.*

#### 1. The Yield Divergence (The PIIGS Crisis)
- Prior to 2009, financial markets priced Greek and German sovereign debt almost identically, assuming the Eurozone implied a de facto mutualized guarantee.
- By 2011-2012, this assumption shattered. The spread between Italian/Spanish 10-year bonds and German Bunds exploded to >500 bps. Greek 10-year yields exceeded 30%.
- **Redenomination Risk**: Investors demanded massive risk premiums because they feared Greece or Italy would exit the Euro, reintroduce their legacy currencies (Drachma/Lira), and instantly devalue the debt.

#### 2. The ISDA Credit Event & CDS
- When Greece effectively defaulted via Private Sector Involvement (PSI) haircuts in 2012, it triggered payout on Sovereign Credit Default Swaps (CDS).
- However, the "CDS Basis" (the mathematical relationship between cash bond yields and CDS premiums) dislocated severely because the banks selling the CDS protection were themselves at risk of bankruptcy.

#### 1. Fiat Sovereignty vs. Currency Pegs
- A sovereign nation that issues debt in its own fiat currency (e.g., US, UK, Japan) can *technically* never be forced into nominal default, because the central bank can always print money to retire the debt.
- The Eurozone crisis occurred because member states surrendered their printing presses to the European Central Bank (ECB). Consequently, Spain and Italy were functionally equivalent to emerging markets borrowing in a "foreign" currency. They could face a true liquidity run.

#### 2. The Power of Credible Central Bank Forward Guidance
