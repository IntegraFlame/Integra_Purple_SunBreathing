### PASS 1: KNOWLEDGE (NEJI EYE / CHAMELEON LENS)
*Objective: Extract structural boundaries, hard mathematical constants, and liquidity transmission channels.*

#### 1. The Fiscal & Monetary Interventions (Late 2008)
- **TARP (Troubled Asset Relief Program)**: A $700 billion US Treasury fund originally designed to buy toxic MBS, but pivoted to directly inject Tier 1 equity capital into major banks (Capital Purchase Program).
- **CPFF (Commercial Paper Funding Facility)**: Fed created an SPV to purchase highly-rated, unsecured and asset-backed commercial paper directly from eligible issuers, bypassing the frozen interbank market.
- **TALF (Term Asset-Backed Securities Loan Facility)**: Fed provided non-recourse loans to investors to buy AAA-rated ABS (auto, student, credit card loans), restarting consumer credit origination.
- **TSLF (Term Securities Lending Facility)**: Allowed primary dealers to pledge illiquid MBS as collateral in exchange for highly liquid US Treasury securities.

#### 2. Quantitative Easing 1 (QE1)
- Initiated in November 2008, expanded in March 2009.
- Total scale: The Fed purchased $1.25 trillion in Agency MBS, $200 billion in Agency debt, and $300 billion in long-term Treasuries.
- Balance Sheet Impact: The Fed's balance sheet exploded from roughly $900 billion (August 2008) to $2.1 trillion (mid-2010).

#### The Mechanics of Quantitative Easing
QE is fundamentally an **asset swap**. The Fed creates digital reserves out of thin air and uses them to buy interest-bearing bonds from the private sector.
1. **Signaling Channel**: Convinces the market the Fed will keep rates at zero for a very long time.
2. **Portfolio Balance Channel**: By removing duration and risk from the private sector (buying MBS/Treasuries), the Fed forces investors who sold those bonds to redeploy their cash into riskier assets (corporate credit, equities) to find yield.

#### 1. The Death of Bagehot's Dictum
Walter Bagehot (1873) outlined the classic central banking rule for a crisis: *"Lend freely to solvent firms, against good collateral, at a penalty rate."*
In 2008, Bernanke's Fed inverted this: **They lent to functionally insolvent firms, against toxic collateral, at subsidized rates.** 
Why? Because the interconnectedness of derivatives and shadow banking meant that the insolvency of one node (AIG, Citi) guaranteed the illiquidity of all nodes. Systemic topology superseded individual solvency.

#### 2. The Great Liquidity Transformation
The fundamental cause of the 2008 crisis was the private sector's attempt to engage in massive maturity transformation (funding 30-year mortgages with 30-day paper). When the private sector failed, the Federal Reserve stepped in to become the ultimate shadow bank, moving the maturity transformation risk onto the sovereign balance sheet.
